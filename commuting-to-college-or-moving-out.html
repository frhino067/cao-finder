Commuting to college or moving out: how to compare your options

The right college choice needs to fit the days between lectures too. Compare the full week, not just a map distance or a single rent figure.

CFBy CAO Finder 19 September 2026 · 3 min read
IN THIS GUIDE
  1. Test the journey door to door
  2. Turn minutes into a weekly comparison
  3. Build a cost sheet from current quotes
  4. Check the parts of the timetable that can change
  5. Make the decision about fit, not the college image

THE TAKEAWAY

Write down time, practical constraints and the costs you have actually checked. A short-looking journey can be very different at the times you need to travel.

Test the journey door to door

Choose a plausible morning arrival time and a later finish. Look up the current route, transfers and the walk from the stop to the actual teaching building. If practical, try the journey at the relevant time. Add a note about what happens when a connection is missed.

Measure both directions. A convenient morning service may not solve getting home after a lab, society event or evening session. Check weekend options if returning home regularly is part of your plan.

Turn minutes into a weekly comparison

Here is an invented example: 70 minutes each way, on four campus days, is 560 minutes—nine hours and twenty minutes of weekly travel. A 25-minute journey each way on the same schedule is 200 minutes, or three hours and twenty minutes.

The six-hour difference is not automatically a reason to move. It is a way to make the trade-off visible. Consider how reliably you can use travel time, what you carry and whether you would still have space for study and other commitments.

Build a cost sheet from current quotes

CommutingMoving closer
Transport fares or vehicle costsAccommodation and any deposit
Food bought on campusFood and household essentials
Occasional alternative travelUtilities if not included
Course equipmentLocal transport and trips home

Keep one-off costs separate from weekly or monthly costs. Check the length of any accommodation arrangement and what the quoted price includes. Do not compare a weekly travel estimate with a monthly rent figure without converting them to the same period.

Check the parts of the timetable that can change

Ask the college whether the programme uses more than one campus, has early practical sessions or includes placements away from the main site. Request an illustrative timetable, while recognising that it may change.

For each option, write a fallback: missed connection, timetable change, an unavailable room or a placement in another location. You do not need a perfect answer to every scenario, but an option with no workable fallback deserves more research.

Make the decision about fit, not the college image

Discuss the realistic costs and travel with whoever will support the decision. Compare the course itself too: moving closer to a programme you do not want does not fix the course mismatch.

Finish with a small decision sheet: strongest benefit, biggest constraint, unresolved question and next action for each option. If the figures are still estimates, label them. Before making commitments, verify current prices, availability and terms with the relevant providers.

Use location filters to widen your course search. You may find another programme worth investigating that changes the travel equation completely.

ONE MORE THING

Is commuting always cheaper?

Not necessarily. Compare your actual travel costs and schedule with accommodation, utilities and trips home. The answer depends on your circumstances and current quotes.
