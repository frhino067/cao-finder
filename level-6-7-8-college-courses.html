Level 6, 7 and 8 courses in Ireland: what is the difference?

The qualification level tells you something useful about the award. It does not, on its own, tell you which course will suit you or whether progression is guaranteed.

THE TAKEAWAY

Compare the award, the actual course and the written progression conditions. Do not assume that a similar title means an automatic route into the next year.

The basic qualification map

For the higher education awards commonly found in CAO searches, the QQI framework identifies the following:

NFQ levelAwardUsual full-time duration
6Higher CertificateTwo years
7Ordinary Bachelor DegreeThree years
8Honours Bachelor DegreeThree or four years; some are longer

Level 6 also includes further education Advanced Certificates. Those are different awards from Higher Certificates. Check the full award name, not just the number.

Compare what you would actually learn

Start with compulsory modules, practical work and assessment. If two programmes share a subject area, compare how far each takes that subject and what projects you would complete. Do not assume a shorter programme is simply the first part of another one.

Next, check whether the award meets any requirements attached to the career or later course you have in mind. Ask the relevant institution or professional body when recognition matters. A broad statement about a qualification level is not a substitute for that check.

Ask these questions about progression

  • What exact course and year can graduates apply to enter?
  • Is there a named arrangement, or only a possibility of applying?
  • What grades and modules are required?
  • Are places limited or competitive?
  • Would the next stage require a move to another campus?
  • How many years would the full route take?

Write the answers beside each route. QQI describes add-on awards within the framework, but that does not establish the conditions for your particular college and course.

Compare a direct route with a staged route

Imagine a student weighing a direct honours degree against a Higher Certificate followed by further study. The staged option might offer a useful earlier award or a convenient initial location. It might also depend on grades, available places and a later move.

Build two timelines showing study years, application points and any uncertain transition. This is an invented decision exercise, not a claim that a particular route exists. Ask the college to confirm the real version before relying on it.

Keep both CAO lists useful

The Level 8 list and Level 7/6 list are independent, as the CAO preference guide explains. Research meaningful options for each rather than filling a list with courses you would not attend.

When searching CAO Finder, change the qualification filter to explore beyond the default Level 8 selection. Some entries remain labelled Level 6/7 where their precise level has not been confirmed in the dataset. Use the college’s current course page to resolve that detail.

ONE MORE THING

Is a Level 7 course automatically a route to Level 8?

No. A progression or add-on route must be checked for that specific programme, including the grades, modules, application process and available places.
