How to use Leaving Cert past papers without wasting them

Doing a paper is only the first half of the job. The useful part is working out why you lost marks and whether you can fix the same mistake next time.

CFBy CAO Finder 19 September 2026 · 3 min read
IN THIS GUIDE
  1. Choose a question with a purpose
  2. Try before opening the marking scheme
  3. Classify the mistake, then choose the repair
  4. Keep an error log that takes one minute
  5. Use full papers to practise the whole performance

THE TAKEAWAY

Attempt, mark, diagnose and redo. Keep a short error log so each paper changes what you study next.

Choose a question with a purpose

Before opening a paper, decide what you are testing: understanding a topic, recalling material without notes, interpreting a question or working within time. Those are different jobs. Early in a topic, an untimed question can be more useful than a whole paper you cannot finish.

Use the State Examinations Commission’s exam material archive for official papers and marking schemes. Check the year, subject and level. Ask your teacher which older papers still match the syllabus and assessment arrangements for your exam year.

Try before opening the marking scheme

Write an actual answer, including the steps you are unsure about. If you get stuck, note the point where you stopped. Reading a solution and thinking “I would have done that” gives you much less information than seeing what you produce on your own.

Keep hints separate. If you needed a formula reminder or a paragraph outline, label that attempt as supported. You can still learn from it, but it should not be mistaken for independent exam performance.

Classify the mistake, then choose the repair

What happened?What to do next
I did not know the conceptRevisit the lesson and attempt a simpler question
I knew it but misread the taskUnderline the command and rephrase what it asks
My answer lacked developmentAdd a specific explanation or supporting example
I made a calculation errorLocate the first wrong step, then redo the working
I ran out of timePractise a shorter timed section and review pacing

Do not record only “lost 12 marks”. That does not tell you what tomorrow’s session should contain.

Keep an error log that takes one minute

Use four columns: question, mistake, repair and retest date. A useful entry might read: “Differentiation question; forgot the chain rule; do two short chain-rule questions; retry on Thursday.”

When you return, start from a blank page. If you reproduce the method independently, move on to a different question that uses the same skill. If you cannot, bring the exact sticking point to your teacher. For extended writing, ask for feedback when you cannot confidently interpret the marking criteria yourself.

Use full papers to practise the whole performance

Once you can handle individual sections, practise choosing questions, allocating time and checking work across a full paper. Follow the instructions for that paper; do not assume an old choice structure applies to your current examination.

Never treat repeated topics as a guarantee of what will appear. Past papers are evidence about your preparation, not a promise about the next exam. End each practice session with one concrete improvement task.

ONE MORE THING

Should I memorise marking-scheme answers?

Use a scheme to understand what earns credit, then practise applying the knowledge to a differently worded question. Copying a model answer is not the same as being able to answer independently.
