Higher or Ordinary Level Maths? A practical decision checklist

This decision deserves more than “Higher has bonus points”. Look at the courses you want, the work required and the grades you can realistically demonstrate.

  5. Set a review point rather than drifting

THE TAKEAWAY

Check course eligibility first, compare realistic best-six totals second, and discuss the learning workload with your maths teacher.

Check whether your target courses need Higher Maths

Make a small table of the courses you are seriously considering and copy their exact maths requirements from the current college pages. Record the course code and application year. If a requirement is unclear, ask the college admissions office rather than guessing.

A large overall points score does not replace a missing required subject grade. Equally, do not assume every course in a broad area has identical requirements. Start with evidence for your actual shortlist.

Understand what the bonus does—and does not do

The CAO maths bonus rules award 25 additional points for H6 or better in Higher Maths. The adjusted maths score must be in your best six to contribute. The bonus is not a separate pass in maths and does not change the grade printed on your result.

As a simple comparison, H6 gives 71 including the bonus, while O1 gives 56 on the standard grid. But those are two different possible performances, not proof that one level is right for you.

Compare complete scenarios, not isolated scores

Write down a realistic grade for every subject under each option, then calculate both best-six totals. Label the estimates as uncertain. Consider whether extra maths work would replace time you need for other subjects, or whether the challenge is manageable with a better plan.

Do not compare your most optimistic Higher result with your most pessimistic Ordinary result. Use teacher feedback, independent attempts and recent work. If the outcome changes dramatically when one assumed grade changes, that uncertainty should be part of the discussion.

Bring useful evidence to your teacher

  • Two recent independent pieces of work, including your working.
  • The topics where you lose the most marks.
  • The maths requirements of three courses you like.
  • A realistic account of your weekly homework and revision time.
  • Your questions about what catching up would involve.

Ask, “Which gaps would I need to close to reach this grade, and how will we check progress?” That is more actionable than asking for a prediction without showing your work.

Set a review point rather than drifting

If you decide to continue at Higher Level for now, agree on specific topics to work on and a date to review progress. If you change level, ask what material and exam technique you need to learn for the new course. Neither option should mean stopping maths practice.

Check school and examination procedures before changing level. Keep your course shortlist current, because a decision that suits your workload can affect which entry requirements you meet. Make the choice with your teacher and guidance counsellor, not with a points comparison alone.

ONE MORE THING

Is staying at Higher Level always worth it for the bonus?

No. The answer depends on the grade you can achieve, whether maths enters your best six, the requirements of your chosen courses and the effect on the rest of your work.
