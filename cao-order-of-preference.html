CAO order of preference: how to rank your courses properly

Your CAO list should express what you want to study. It is not a prediction of what points you will get, and it is not a league table of colleges.

THE TAKEAWAY

Put the course you genuinely want most first. Use historical points to explore options, not to decide the order of your preferences.

Keep preference and prediction separate

CAO advises applicants to rank courses in genuine order of preference. Its Level 8 and Level 7/6 lists are independent. Each list has space for up to ten choices.

Write two different questions at the top of your research page: “Would I want this course?” and “What do I need to qualify?” They both deserve attention, but they are not interchangeable. Feeling unsure about your predicted points does not tell you whether you prefer one course over another.

An example of a preference list

Imagine you prefer Course A to Course B, and Course B to Course C. Their previous points were 510, 460 and 480 respectively. Your order should still be A, B, C. The figures do not move C above B.

These are fictional courses and points. The example is useful because it strips away prestige, parental expectations and the temptation to arrange everything numerically. If the points were hidden, which offer would you be happiest to open?

Use a pair-by-pair check

  1. Compare choices one and two. If both were available, which would you take?
  2. Repeat for two and three, then continue down the list.
  3. For any difficult pair, compare compulsory modules and the practical college week.
  4. Move a course only when your preference changes, then recheck its neighbours.

A tie often means you are missing information. Find the missing detail: perhaps one course has a compulsory language, a different campus, or a placement arrangement you have not checked. Resolve that question before trying to force a ranking.

Understand what a later offer can change

CAO’s offer examples explain that an offer removes lower preferences on that same list from further consideration. Higher preferences can remain in play; accepting an earlier offer does not itself prevent a later higher-preference offer. Always read the reply deadline and instructions on your actual offer.

This is why a course you would never accept should not sit above one you really want. It is also why you should judge an actual offer carefully rather than assume another will arrive.

A final check before you save changes

  • Read every course code beside its title and campus.
  • Check the qualification level and all compulsory requirements.
  • Make sure every choice represents an option you would consider taking.
  • Recheck both lists, not just the Level 8 list.
  • Confirm the changes were saved in your official CAO account.

CAO Finder’s saved list is a research tool. It does not submit an application or set your official preference order. Keep a dated personal copy of your intended order so you can compare it with the account later.

ONE MORE THING

Should I put a lower-points course first to be safe?

Put it first if it is your favourite. A previous points figure should not be the reason you place it above a course you prefer.
