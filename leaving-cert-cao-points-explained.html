Leaving Cert points explained: calculate your best six correctly

A points total is useful only if you calculate it correctly. Here is a worked example you can copy, including the subject that drops out of the best six.

THE TAKEAWAY

Convert grades to points, add any eligible Higher Maths bonus to maths, then take the six highest scores from one sitting.

The Higher and Ordinary Level points table

The standard grade values below come from the official CAO scoring grid. The six best recognised subject results from one sitting count. You must also meet course entry requirements.

A seven-subject example: 467 points

This is an invented example, not a predicted set of results. Suppose a student gets English H2, Irish O2, Maths H4, Economics H3, Geography H3, Biology H4 and French H4.

SubjectScoreCounted?

English H288Yes
Economics H377Yes

91 + 88 + 77 + 77 + 66 + 66 = 467. Irish is still relevant to any applicable entry requirement even though it does not contribute to this total.

Why the maths bonus goes in before sorting

An eligible H6 or better in Higher Maths receives 25 extra points. Maths contributes to the total only if its adjusted score is among the best six. See CAO’s maths bonus examples.

Use a column for each subject’s score rather than adding 25 to the final total automatically. That makes it much easier to see whether maths actually displaced another subject.

Turn a total into a planning tool

Make three versions of your estimate: grades you have already demonstrated consistently, a reasonable improvement, and a stretch scenario. Label them clearly. A good mock or difficult class test should not become a guarantee about the final exam.

Change one grade at a time and recalculate. In the example above, moving Economics from H3 to H2 would add 11 points. Improving Irish from O2 to O1 would not change the best-six total in that example, although the result could still matter for other reasons.

Know what the calculator does not cover

CAO Finder’s calculator handles Higher and Ordinary Level grades and the maths bonus. It does not model LCVP, Foundation Level or medicine’s adjusted scoring. Use official guidance for those cases.

After calculating, check individual course requirements and the year shown beside historical points. A total helps you explore; it is not an offer or an eligibility decision.

ONE MORE THING

Does a seventh subject add extra points?

Only the best six count towards the standard total. A seventh subject can enter that six if its score is high enough, and can also matter for entry requirements.
