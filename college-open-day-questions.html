Choosing a course

College open day questions: a checklist that goes beyond the brochure

An open day is a chance to test whether a course fits your real week. Arrive with a shortlist and leave with answers you can compare.

THE TAKEAWAY

Ask for examples of the actual student experience: a compulsory module, a typical week, an assignment and the practical details of any placement.

Choose three questions before you travel

Read the official course page first and identify what it does not answer. If the modules are already listed, use your time to ask how they are taught or assessed. If a placement is advertised, ask how it is organised.

Pick your three most important questions and save them on your phone. Check the event schedule, campus and registration instructions directly with the college before travelling. Give yourself time to move between buildings instead of booking sessions back to back.

Questions to ask teaching staff

  • Which first-year module do new students find most surprising, and why?
  • Can you describe a typical assignment?
  • Which modules are compulsory, and when do students choose options?
  • How much work involves maths, writing, practical tasks or group projects?
  • What happens if an optional module has more applicants than places?
  • Who should I contact to confirm a particular entry requirement?

These questions turn a general pitch into something you can imagine doing. Listen for concrete examples and note any answer that needs a follow-up link.

Questions to ask current students

Ask what their week looks like, what they wish they had known and how they manage travel between classes. “How did you find your first group project?” is often easier to answer than “Is the course good?”

One student’s account is a perspective, not a guarantee. Compare it with official information and, where possible, another student’s experience. Avoid treating a very positive or very negative story as the whole picture.

Check the practical details

AreaQuestion worth asking
PlacementIs it compulsory, who finds it, and where might it be?
CampusAre all years taught here?
TimetableCan I see an illustrative week, including labs?
SupportHow do students access learning and accessibility support?
CostsAre there course-specific equipment or travel costs?

An illustrative timetable can change. Use it to uncover questions about your commute or commitments, not to assume next year will be identical.

Do a ten-minute debrief before the details blur

Write three things you liked, two concerns and one action. For example: “Liked the project work; worried about the placement location; email the coordinator for details.” Keep course-specific notes separate from your impression of the campus.

Then compare the visit with your other shortlisted courses. A lively atmosphere is a bonus, but the modules and practical commitments will shape far more of your week. If you cannot attend, use a virtual event or ask admissions for the same information.

Finally, change your shortlist only where the evidence changed your view. You do not need to make a decision on the train home.

ONE MORE THING

What should I bring to an open day?

Your course codes, three priority questions, a way to take notes and a travel plan. Check the college’s event instructions for anything else you need.
